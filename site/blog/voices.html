Giving it a voice

June 2026 · notes/03

The voice is the product. A reader you'll actually listen to for twenty minutes lives or dies on whether the voice sounds like a person or a navigation system. readback uses CSM-1B, Sesame's conversational speech model, and the interesting part is how it decides what to sound like.

## Voices are reference clips

CSM doesn't have a fixed voice. It conditions on a short reference clip — the audio plus its exact transcript — and reproduces that clip's timbre, age, and accent in everything it says next. readback ships two built-in reading voices, but the fun is in the third option: drop a clean 5–8 second clip of any voice into the config, and that's your reader.

tts:
  csm:
    speaker: "kay"
    temperature: 0.6        # delivery: lower = composed, higher = livelier
    voices:
      - name: "kay"
        wav: "src/voice/voice_kay_long.wav"
        ref_text: "Exact transcript of the clip."

## What actually matters

  • The transcript must match the audio, exactly. A mismatched (audio, text) pair is the number-one cause of a garbled clone — the model learns the wrong mapping for the voice.
  • Clip length buys stability. Below ~5 seconds of reference, the clone destabilizes at low temperature — it rambles and repeats. The sample voice that ships with readback was bootstrapped from a 3-second clip into an 11-second one for exactly this reason.
  • Temperature tunes delivery, not identity. Lower reads composed and measured; higher reads livelier. Who it sounds like comes from the clip.

## When a clip isn't enough

Clone-conditioning gets you remarkably far for one clip and zero training. For higher fidelity there's a LoRA fine-tune pipeline in the repo: transcribe a longer set of clips, convert them to the training format, and train a small adapter that's loaded over the base weights. The pipeline is tuned to run on a consumer Mac (batch size 1, gradient accumulation, checkpointing) — the same machine that does the reading can train its own voice.

Hear the result on the landing page — the sample read is kay, a clone-conditioned voice.
