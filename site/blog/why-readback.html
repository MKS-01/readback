Why I built readback

June 2026 · notes/01

I read a lot, but there's a whole pile of good writing I never get to. Some days I'd just rather listen than read another wall of text — on a walk, while cooking, with my eyes closed. Podcasts cover a sliver of it; the long essays, the technical deep-dives, the blog posts friends send me — those just pile up in tabs.

So I built readback to do exactly one thing: paste a URL, get a clean, natural-voice reading of the whole article, right in the terminal.

## A decade-old itch

I've been tinkering with voice agents since college, back in 2015 — when it was a basic speech-to-text → text-to-speech loop held together with duct tape. readback is that same itch leveled up. In fact, this project started as a real-time voice assistant: microphone capture, voice activity detection, wake-words, the whole live cascade. Building that taught me something useful — the part I actually wanted wasn't the conversation. It was the listening. So I cut everything else, and the assistant became a reader.

## Why offline, why terminal

Everything runs on-device on Apple Silicon: a local LLM through Ollama for summaries, and CSM-1B — a genuinely good neural voice — through MLX on the GPU. No accounts, no API keys, nothing phoning home. That's not a privacy checkbox so much as a property I wanted the tool to have: it works on a plane, it costs nothing per read, and an article I feed it stays on my machine.

And it lives in the terminal because that's where it always felt most at home. No browser tab, no electron app — a prompt, a URL, and a player with pause, seek, and a transcript that follows the voice word by word.

Made to make reading interesting again.
